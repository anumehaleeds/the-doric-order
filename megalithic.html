Introduction

Megalithic architecture refers to the construction of large stone structures or monuments using massive stones, often without the use of mortar or other binding materials. These structures are typically associated with prehistoric societies. The term "megalith" itself, derived from the Greek words "mega" (meaning large) and "lithos" (meaning stone), aptly captures the essence of this architectural form.

Orientation

Many megalithic structures exhibit specific alignments or orientations. Some are aligned with astronomical events, such as the solstices or equinoxes, suggesting possible astronomical or calendrical significance. Building megalithic structures included methods for quarrying, transporting, and lifting the massive stones. The exact techniques used remain a subject of study and speculation.
